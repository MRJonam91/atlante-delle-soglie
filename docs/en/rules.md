# Rules · 0.1.0-playtest

One facilitator prepares situations and consequences; 3–5 players decide what their Keepers do. The facilitator does not decide their thoughts. Agree on duration and intensity before play. Anyone may request a pause or change a theme without explaining why. Character fears never require personal disclosures.

## Create a Keeper

Choose a name, imaginary origin, wish, fear, promise, keepsake and bond with another Keeper. Assign **+3, +2, +1, +0** once each to Courage, Cunning, Wonder and Bond.

| Quality | Examples |
|---|---|
| Courage | Protecting, enduring, force and weapons |
| Cunning | Searching, building, precise movement |
| Wonder | Understanding and changing magic |
| Bond | Listening, persuading, coordinating |

Start with **6 Energy**, **3 Embers**, two ordinary objects and one talent from the [ready-made characters](characters.md). Origins describe fiction: flight or a wooden body does not grant unlimited access or immunity. Agree on limits for unusual abilities before play.

## Checks and consequences

When an action is uncertain and has consequences, the facilitator states the difficulty and risk. Roll **d20 + relevant quality**. Meeting or exceeding the difficulty succeeds. Difficulties: 10 simple under pressure, 13 ordinary, 16 difficult, 19 exceptional.

Do not roll for obvious, safe actions. Essential clues remain available after failure, with a cost or complication. Repeating an unchanged action without a new approach or situation does not permit more rolls.

Advantage: roll two d20s and keep the higher. Disadvantage: keep the lower. Multiple sources do not stack; advantage and disadvantage cancel. A natural 20 adds a small benefit if the check succeeds; a natural 1 adds no automatic punishment.

## Resources

Personal **Embers** power talents, maximum 3. Every talent costs one Ember unless explicitly stated otherwise. Shared **Sparks** start at 3, maximum 6. After seeing a check's result but before applying its consequence, spend one Spark for +2 with the rolling player's consent. Maximum one Spark per check.

A scene is a situation with one objective and danger; leaving and returning does not reset it. Award at most one Spark per scene for keeping a costly promise, reconciling two parties or completing an objective together. Specific adventure rewards use this same limit. Helping does not award a Spark every time.

## Zones, actions and reactions

A zone is an area for close interaction. Map connections are passages. When timing matters, each round has a hero phase, an opponent/environment phase and any timer advance.

Each hero takes one turn in an agreed order: **one move along one connection and one action**, in either order. Actions include attack, maneuver, help, activate something, persuade, or move along another connection. Brief speech is free; achieving a decisive result through dialogue costs an action.

**Help costs an action** and grants advantage on an ally's next relevant check before your next turn begins. The assistance must be possible; only one helper per check.

A reaction is available only when a talent permits it and its trigger occurs. Maximum **one reaction per hero per round**, including outside the hero phase. Outside conflict, each talent use applies to one check or consequence and still costs an Ember; no repeated uses on the same check.

## Conflicts and maneuvers

Attack with a quality matching the means: Courage for force, Cunning for precision, Wonder for magic. Bond can influence a creature that understands the approach; it does not automatically damage structures, machines or objects.

Melee reaches your zone. Ranged attacks reach a visible adjacent zone if the means allow it. Every hero agrees on an ordinary means of attack at the start. A simple magical attack can be ordinary and cost no Ember; special effects belong to talents.

Roll against **Guard**. On success choose **2 Impact**, or **1 Impact and one condition**. Subtract Impact from **Stability**. At zero, the obstacle is overcome as described, such as disarmed or deactivated; injury is optional narration. Obstacles state any condition limits.

Opponents never roll. During its activation each does **one thing**: attack a hero already in reach, or move one zone. It cannot move and attack in the same activation. The target rolls the listed defense; failure causes the stated Energy loss. There are no opportunity attacks.

## Conditions

| Condition | Hero | Opponent |
|---|---|---|
| Hindered | Loses the next free move, then ends; an action can still provide a move | Loses its next attempted move, then ends; otherwise expires at the end of its next activation |
| Shaken | Disadvantage on the next check, then ends | The next hero defending against its attack has advantage, then ends; without an attack it expires at the end of its next activation |

Duplicates never extend a condition. Shaken does not also grant advantage on attacks against the opponent. Hindered does not prevent an attack that already required no movement.

## Trouble, rescue and rest

At zero Energy you are **in trouble**: on your turn you may speak and move one zone, but have no action, talent or reaction. Further Energy loss does nothing. An ally in your zone may spend an action to restore you to 2 Energy, once per recipient per scene. A healing talent can also help at zero but does not reset an already used rescue.

If everyone is in trouble, apply the adventure's defeat outcome. In the following scene each hero returns with 3 Energy and their remaining Embers. There is no automatic death rule.

One safe ten-minute break restores 2 Energy and 1 Ember, up to the maximums. Only one break per short adventure, never during a timed scene. Returning to the Library between adventures restores everything. Repeating breaks cannot farm resources.

## Growth and complexity

This version has one power level. Record a memory, a changed relationship and a promise after play. Numerical advancement needs further testing.

For beginners, suggest two possible actions while welcoming other ideas. Experienced groups can explore dilemmas and persistent consequences with the same rules. Cover, elevation and combined magic are future modules, not surprise rules introduced during a conflict.
